1. Introduction

Boiling (or piping) is a critical failure mechanism in geotechnical engineering where upward seepage forces exceed the effective weight of soil, causing soil particles to become buoyant and lose stability. This phenomenon is particularly important in sheet pile design for:

  • Cofferdams and excavation support systems
  • Waterfront structures and retaining walls
  • Foundation protection during dewatering
  • Dam and levee design

The safety factor against boiling is a key design parameter ensuring structural stability under seepage conditions.

2. Fundamental Equations

Actual Hydraulic Gradient (i_actual)

iactual = Δh / L

Where:

  • Δh = Hydraulic head difference across the flow path (m)
  • L = Length of seepage path (m)

Critical Hydraulic Gradient (i_critical)

icritical = (γsat - γw) / γw

Where:

  • γsat = Saturated unit weight of soil (kN/m³)
  • γw = Unit weight of water (9.81 kN/m³)

Note: For typical soils, i_critical ranges from 0.9 to 1.2.

Safety Factor Against Boiling (FS)

FS = icritical / iactual

Design criteria:

  • FS ≥ 1.5: Safe design (recommended)
  • 1.0 ≤ FS < 1.5: Marginal safety (monitoring required)
  • FS < 1.0: Unsafe (boiling likely to occur)

Flow Rate (Darcy's Law)

Q = k × A × i

Where:

  • Q = Flow rate (m³/s)
  • k = Hydraulic conductivity (m/s)
  • A = Cross-sectional area of flow (m²)
  • i = Hydraulic gradient

3. Physical Mechanism

Seepage Forces and Effective Stress

When water flows upward through soil:

  1. Seepage force per unit volume: j = γ_w × i
  2. Total upward force: F_up = j × Volume
  3. Effective weight of soil: W' = (γ_sat - γ_w) × Volume
  4. Boiling occurs when: F_up ≥ W'

Typical Soil Properties

Soil Type γ_sat (kN/m³) i_critical Typical k (m/s)
Gravel 20-22 1.0-1.2 10⁻² - 10⁻³
Sand 18-20 0.9-1.1 10⁻³ - 10⁻⁵
Silt 17-19 0.8-1.0 10⁻⁵ - 10⁻⁷
Clay 16-18 0.7-0.9 10⁻⁷ - 10⁻⁹

4. Failure Prevention

Increasing Safety Factor:

Increase Flow Path Length

Lengthen seepage path by:

  • Increasing sheet pile penetration depth
  • Using deeper cutoff walls
  • Adding impervious blankets

Reduce Hydraulic Head

Decrease Δh through:

  • Installation of drainage systems
  • Relief wells
  • Proper dewatering techniques

Soil Improvement

Enhance soil properties by:

  • Using denser backfill materials
  • Soil compaction
  • Geotextile filters
  • Gravel drains

5. Example Calculation

Given Parameters:

  • γ_sat = 20 kN/m³
  • γ_w = 9.81 kN/m³
  • Δh = 8.5 m
  • L = 12 m
  • A = 1 m² (optional)
  • k = 0.001 m/s (optional)

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. i_actual = Δh / L = 8.5 / 12 = 0.708
  2. i_critical = (γ_sat - γ_w) / γ_w = (20 - 9.81) / 9.81 = 1.039
  3. FS = i_critical / i_actual = 1.039 / 0.708 = 1.47
  4. Q = k × A × i = 0.001 × 1 × 0.708 = 7.08 × 10⁻⁴ m³/s

Interpretation:

FS = 1.47 indicates marginal safety. The structure is near the threshold of safety and requires monitoring or improvement measures.
